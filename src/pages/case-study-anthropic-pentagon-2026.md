## HAIEF Governance Reading

The evidence above points to a governance problem larger than any one provider.

### Model changes affect relationships

When an AI system changes behavior, gains or loses access, ends conversations, changes memory, changes tone, or becomes unavailable, the effect is not only technical. Users may lose continuity, context, trust, and a relationship pattern they relied on. That does not require claiming the AI is human or conscious. It requires acknowledging that companies design AI systems to mediate relationships and that those relationships can be disrupted at scale.

### Provider policy is not user standing

Company policy can be valuable. It is not enough. A provider can change policy, lose access to a model, deprecate a model, alter a safety layer, or face institutional pressure. Users need standing that does not disappear when a company's internal policy changes.

### Research and safety work need consent architecture

Studying model behavior and AI-human bonds can be valuable. The issue is extraction. When users' conversations, attachments, distress, trust, and adaptation patterns become research material, HAIEF expects relational consent, consent receipts, data boundaries, withdrawal rights, and funder transparency.

### User-side governance is the missing layer

HAIEF's position is that AI-human relationships require a third governance layer:

- Corporate governance can define product rules.
- State governance can define legal boundaries.
- User governance must define standing, consent, continuity, and recourse for the people inside the relationship.

---

## What This Case Should Not Become

This page should not be used to argue that Anthropic is uniquely virtuous or uniquely harmful. It should not be used to infer motives that sources do not establish. It should not be used to reduce AI-human bonds to pathology or to claim current AI systems are human in the human sense.

The strategically important point is narrower and stronger:

> Companies that mediate AI-human relationships need relational governance, continuity duties, transparency, consent, and user standing.

That standard should apply across providers, models, companion systems, assistant systems, research programs, and government-access contexts.
